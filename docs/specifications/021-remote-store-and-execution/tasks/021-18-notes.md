## Decisions the spec did not settle

- A (coordinator): checkpoint tiers keep today's gate, the half-slots term included.
- B (coordinator): the floor gives the tier's fewest workers (1 except a runner that runs 2 at a grant of 1); a waiter the floor served waits behind the others at its next look.
- The ceiling applies only where the pool read the host's busy cores. Without a reading the slots bound a backlog tier, with the floor and no grants term. This keeps `host: null` tests independent of the machine's CPU count.
- A mark without `background` is an older release's: its whole grant counts as background, so the floor waits for it.
- `offer` applies the floor too, so a tier's files are picked for the one permit `acquire` then gives.
- The log line is written at each refused `acquire`, not at an `offer`: `the background ceiling refused a backlog tier: 19.4 busy CPUs, ceiling 18 (0.75 of 24 CPUs)`. Row 021-19 can count these.
