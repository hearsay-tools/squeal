## Blockers

### B1. Proven: a claim that ends without a result leaves the promotion unable to yield its tier

**Location:** `src/core/scheduler/yields.ts:254` (`waitedFor`), with `#decide` at line 159 and `boundOf` at line 329. The busy-lane wait in `src/core/scheduler/scheduler.ts:795` explains why the normal claim wake does not repair it. This is the regression named by 011-123.

**Contract:** C15: “A promoted file queued behind a running tier makes it yield as C4 step 5 says: once per file and key, when its estimated remainder exceeds 30 s, never the backlog's turn.” The new exception applies to a file that **waits on** another worktree's claim. C7 says that claim ends when the claimant records, its heartbeat expires, or the waiter's timeout plus 6 s expires (600 s with no timeout). Once a claim ends without a result, the exception no longer holds.

**Failure:** Promote F while a live foreign claim holds F's key and an ordinary edit tier runs four files with a 200 s estimate in F's lane. The new filter correctly leaves that tier running. If the claimant then dies without a result, F has no claim to wait for, but nothing rechecks the yield. `waitedFor` previously returned false, so the tier got no timer. The pump waits for an event while every queued lane is busy, before it reaches `claimWake`. Quiet interval passes also do not call `#cutTiers` when they create no revision or runner change.

A 60 s named wait can thus reach its deadline behind the 200 s tier even when the claimant disappears after about 2 s and the tier still has well over 30 s left. With a longer wait, the delay can last until the tier ends or another event rechecks it. It is no longer bounded by the expired claim or by the yield rule. Before this change the initial promotion cut the tier; the file then waited on the foreign claim in a free lane and could run after the claim ended.

**Independent probe:** A temporary test used the candidate's existing `tier-yields-claim.test.ts` helpers and unchanged product code. It reproduced the first test's claimed F and held 200 s tier, promoted only F for 60 s, and confirmed that the tier stayed running. It then cleared the foreign worktree's daemon record without storing a result. Status no longer reported a claim. Five quiet `handleBatch({ trigger: "interval", paths: [] })` passes, 1 s apart, left the tier uncancelled. Re-promoting the same F cancelled it immediately. The probe printed:

```text

Test Files  1 failed (1)

```

Only the throwaway test was added during this diagnostic. It was removed before the final clean-tree check. Its failure is intentional evidence for B1, not a failing committed test. The source paths above establish why it persists past those five passes.

**One-worker fix:** Complete 011-123 before landing 011-55. Recheck yield decisions when a claim ceases to hold, including when the lane is busy. A bounded timer or the existing claim polling cadence must cover a dead claimant as well as the waiter's bound. Keep suppressing the yield while the claim is live. A claimant that stores a usable result must let F take it without a needless cut. Do not require a new edit or a second promotion.
