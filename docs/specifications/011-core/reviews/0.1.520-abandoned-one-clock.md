## S1. Proven: choose whether rollback may suspend progress and safety bounds

Changed cause: `src/core/scheduler/wakes.ts:258` and `:294`; host wiring: `src/core/daemon/daemon.ts:911`. Owner examples: `runner-work-bound.ts:71`, `slot-records.ts:111`, `scheduler.ts:1376`, `quiet.ts:37`.

The implementation correctly follows the requested rule, “a wake is due when `clock.now() >= wake`.” With raw `Date.now`, that makes a wall-clock rollback affect more than when a timer callback runs. It can withhold a completed result from the store and prolong a wedged runner call. This is a deliberate policy choice in R1, not an accidental second clock or a lost callback.

### Re-arm and retention proof

A source probe stated one slot of each of the nine `WAKE_ORDER` reasons at 100, 200, ..., 900 ms. Its timer clock moved normally; an offset changed only `now()` by one hour.

```text
rollback -3600000 ms:
  at 100 ms: no owner fired; exactly one replacement arm
  after advancing to 3600900 ms: all nine owners fired once
  total arms 10; maximum live timers 1; timers left 0
forward +3600000 ms:
  at the first timer, 100 ms: all nine owners fired once in WAKE_ORDER
  total arms 1; maximum live timers 1; timers left 0
```

Together with the candidate's backward/forward cases and late-pass tests, this proves finite re-arming without a busy loop or a permanent stall for one finite jump and a clock that then progresses normally. A backward early pass keeps the slots and arms for the remaining difference. A forward pass consumes all due slots. No pending one-shot wake is skipped. A periodic owner does not replay every interval crossed by a forward jump; it runs and states its next wake. An owner can still clear, move or drop another slot as the existing contract permits.

A forward jump does not itself notify Node's timer. Owners run at the next timer pass, potentially earlier in elapsed time than their original delay; they do not necessarily run at the instant of the jump. Repeated rollbacks can postpone a wake without a finite real-time bound. Event-loop starvation is outside this proof.

### What the user sees

| Wait | Backward jump by D | Forward jump |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet and catch-up quiet | A pending pump recheck can be postponed by D. An otherwise idle lane can wait D plus its remaining 20 s/120 s quiet period. The quiet deadline itself already used wall time before this candidate; the new loss is regular rechecking during the rollback. | The next pass may end the wall-time quiet wait early in elapsed time. |
| Claims window | The pending 250 ms store poll can wait D plus its remainder. Another worktree's completed result can go unnoticed during that interval. The 1 s window/recheck is no longer a real-time polling bound. A separate scheduler event can end the pump's claims wait sooner. | The next poll can find the window expired and resume selection early. Claim age/liveness decisions already used wall time. |
| Slot gather | A finished backlog slot can wait D plus its remaining gather before its result is committed and delivered. With a fresh gather this is 25 ms plus D. Progress can show pending work although the runner already finished. | A pass can end the gather before its original elapsed delay. |
| Holds | The command-hold expiry wake is delayed. Its absolute `until` already depended on wall time. More significantly, merge-hold polling can stop for D even if the index is already released; another check/event may discover the release sooner. | Expired command holds are pruned on the next check; a merge poll may happen early but still checks the index. |
| Bounds and resource waits | Runner-part abandonment, yield-bound checks, slow-runner release and slow-lane/capacity rechecks can all be delayed by D. A runner call holding the scheduler lock can retain it past its advertised bound. | A pass can abandon a call, check a yield bound or release an idle lane before the original elapsed delay. The timer can therefore leave scheduler work idle for an hour after a one-hour rollback, despite the process and independent watcher/heartbeat timers remaining alive. New edits or requests can wake the pump, but they do not guarantee release of an outstanding slot-gather promise or runner-part bound whose wake is still in the future. There is no evidence that the diff changes a result key or makes an old result current. The proven problem is delayed evidence and weakened elapsed-time bounds.

This matters to spec 011 C4's wording: “A runner-part call has a maximum duration” and a wedged call holds the lock “no longer than one bound.” Those statements need qualification or protection if raw wall time is selected. The existing behavior-preservation claim should not imply those bounds stay unchanged in elapsed time.

### A bounded alternative and its limit

A host adapter can expose one monotonically advancing epoch clock to the whole scheduler: capture wall epoch and steady time at construction, then return `epoch + steadyElapsed` from `now()`, with `arm` using the corresponding delay. A backward civil-clock adjustment then cannot extend an existing duration. The loop still has only `now` and `arm`, with no `ticks`, per-slot steady deadline or second pass reading. This also avoids shortening durations on a forward adjustment.

That is an alternative clock policy, not a drop-in claim that all status contracts stay the same. The next worker must check comparisons with other processes' wall-stamped claims, holds, heartbeat and published deadlines. A logical epoch that diverges from the host's current wall time needs an explicit boundary for those facts. Merely freezing `now` at its greatest value is insufficient: it must keep advancing during a rollback.

Capping `hostClock.arm` at the original delay alone does **not** bound the owner's real delay under this candidate. At each capped callback `now < wake`, so `Wakes` fires nothing and re-arms again. Keeping raw wall time for every due decision, requiring `now >= wake`, and bounding real delay independently of rollback cannot all hold. The human must relax one of those requirements.

Worker-sized next step after that choice: pin the host clock policy in `clock.ts` and its tests; add the held-call/finished-slot rollback cases through the selected adapter; retain all nine-owner one-timer checks; verify external timestamp comparisons. If the human instead accepts the current behavior, the coordinator must record it as an amendment, explicitly including runner-part duration bounds and delayed result delivery. Neither decision is supplied by this PASS.
