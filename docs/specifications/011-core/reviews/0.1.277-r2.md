### B1. The snapshot does not bind a hook's step-down to the daemon it read

**Proven.** `src/harness/shared/ensure.ts:157`, after the snapshot ends at `:153`; the request at `:255` and the follow-up `stop` at `:263` address only the reusable socket path. C9's 2026-10-23 amendment says: "No hook asks that incarnation to step down again." Row 011-140 requires that a successor older than its claim gets no second step-down. This is the first review's B1 at the remaining boundary, not a new review of settled work.

The repair reads the daemon record, other sessions' versions, short mark and claim in one read transaction. That closes the original mismatch between an old record and a new mark. The transaction ends before `requestStepDown` opens its socket. A hook paused there can resume after another hook has completed the predecessor's step-down, claimed the handoff, and registered an older successor with a short mark. Its saved record still passes the completed decision. It sends the version-only request to the successor at the same path. On acceptance it sends `stop` on another connection and claims another handoff from its obsolete `from` value.

A scratch probe fixes this ordering rather than relying on load. It uses the candidate's `ensureIfStale`, real store connections and real socket client. The only client shim runs a writer immediately before the first call to the original `requestDaemon`, then forwards the original arguments and result unchanged. The writer atomically sets the successor record and runs `claimHandoff`/`endHandoff`, as registration ends the claim in its transaction. The probe asserts that the short mark names `0.1.272@2#u` before the client connects. The hook snapshot holds `0.1.272@1#t`; the hook version is `0.1.273`. The socket stand-in records both `step-down` and `stop`, and one successor spawn with the lock wait is requested. All those assertions passed. The stand-in accepts the version-only request as the real handler does (`src/core/daemon/handlers.ts:295`); that handler's cancellation and shutdown are read in source, not claimed as an additional real-daemon experiment.
