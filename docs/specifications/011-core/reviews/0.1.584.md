## Blockers

### B1. The smaller share total can prevent the minimum-worker floor forever

**Proven.** Changed boundary: `src/core/capacity/pool.ts:448`, using `backlogShareSlots` from `src/core/capacity/background.ts:34`. The target is now limited by the rounded ceiling before admission considers the floor.

Reproduction: one daemon, an idle 4-CPU host, 3 slots, `backgroundShare: 0.25`, a backlog tier asking for 10 workers with `least: 2`. Its ceiling and share total are 1. The old pool grants 2; the candidate grants 0:

```text
review floor: {"base":2,"candidate":0}
AssertionError: expected +0 to be 2
```

`fairShares(1, [me])` cannot raise a lone share of 1 to a minimum of 2: it has no junior share to borrow. `#gated` correctly offers the two-worker floor, but `acquire` takes `min(target, gated)`, gets 1, and refuses it as below `fewest`. Every later look repeats this. No load or competing daemon is needed. A zero or sub-one ceiling has the same problem when the host has at least two slots. Slow tiers with larger minimum widths can encounter the same boundary.

This breaks the brief's "No change with one daemon" and background progress. C10's Runner ceiling requires an `isolate: false` Vitest tier to ask and wait for 2 permits unless the project sets 1. The existing floor test also states that the oldest waiter receives its tier's fewest workers, but tests this only under a ceiling of 18. Lowering the share to 1 leaves that previously valid tier unable to start. The remote runner's pool has the same regression.

**One-worker fix:** make the new fair-share limit and the existing minimum-worker floor agree. Preserve the oldest waiter's minimum-width admission when no background work runs and enough host/project permits exist, without bypassing waiter order or project caps. Add a regression for an idle lone daemon with `least: 2` and ceilings 0, 0.75 and 1; include a contending waiter and the remote admission caller. A retry or sleep cannot repair this arithmetic.

### B2. A claims wait bypasses withdrawal of restarted quiet demand

**Proven.** Changed boundary: `src/core/scheduler/tier-live.ts:80`. Publication is a side effect of `quietWakeMs`. The caller, unchanged `src/core/scheduler/tier-plan.ts:308`, returns a claims idle answer before reading it.

The independent probe first publishes six held catch-up files in the quiet's last 10 s through the live view and real pool. An edit then calls `quiet.queued`, moving that lane's end back to 120 s. `quietDemand` correctly returns 0. The next actual `planTiers(...).first` sees two lanes: catch-up held in the restarted quiet, and a different lane whose file is claimed by another worktree. It returns `idle: "claims"`. It never calls the live view's `quietWakeMs`, and the old pool mark survives:

```text
review claims: {"idle":"claims","demandAfterRestart":6,"peerOffer":5}
AssertionError: expected 6 to be +0
```

The peer asks against a 9-worker ceiling and is offered 5 instead of 9. No quiet file is due within 30 s. This directly breaks the brief's "an edit withdraws the demand within one pump look" and C10's "until ... the wait ... starts again". Repeated claim looks do not clear it. Without a running tier it can remain until mark freshness expires; with an edit tier active, the normal refresh republishes the stored quiet demand and can keep it fresh. A catch-up quiet's publication can also be missed entirely when claim waits precede its first look in the lead window.

The worker notes disclose the skipped claims branch, but the constraint and spec were not amended to allow it. The committed scheduler test calls `quietWakeMs` itself, so it proves the calculation but cannot catch the caller skipping it.

**One-worker fix:** update quiet demand at one pump/state-update boundary before the choice between a start, claims and quiet idle. Keep the quiet wake calculation free to retain its current priority. Add a test through the tier plan or live scheduler that publishes demand, restarts the quiet, then waits on an unrelated claim; assert the mark is withdrawn and the peer's offer recovers on that look. Cover an edit tier that calls `running`, without `acquire`, so mark refresh cannot preserve obsolete demand.
