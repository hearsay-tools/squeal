# 011-139: the permits and cores edit tiers take on a busy host

Row 011-139, wave 31 R (`../../tasks/wave-31.md`). Measured 2026-10-24, 11:32Z to 12:36Z, on 24 CPUs with `capacity.hostSlots` 16. Every daemon was 0.1.289 or 0.1.291, except one 0.1.267 in toolkit-dev that ran nothing. Code read at `36ee11d02`. This session's worktree (`fab93da1`, id `bcce6c5d81cda799`) is out of every count. The probe is `../probes/host-load/011-139/`.

**Verdict.** Edit tiers held no permit in any sample, and at most 3.1 cores. Do not cap them. Every worktree that held most of the pool ran a backlog or `squeal run --all` tier. The CPU gate (row 011-98) let those tiers grow on a saturated host, because it reads only Squeal's own CPU. Proposed: the gate reads the host's busy cores. A daemon then takes a permit on a busy host only while all the grants are below half the slots. That costs an edit's wait 0 s. It costs a backlog tier about 7 to 9 s at the median (model, 16 workers against 8).

## Method

`sample-pool.py` ran from 11:36:26Z to 12:36:11Z. It took one line every 8 to 16 s, 443 in all; 333 have 18 or more busy cores. A shorter first run (11:32:45Z to 11:34:57Z, 14 samples) gives permits only, because its tree CPU counted a reaped child twice. Permits come from `/proc/locks`: a POSIX write lock on a `pool.<i>.lock` inode, by the holder's pid, matched to a daemon's process tree. A daemon's worktree id is `sha256(realpath(root))[:16]` (`src/core/fs/git-layout.ts:48`), which names its mark. Load is the 1-minute average beside each sample in `samples-2026-10-24.txt`. The width model ran on store copies of 11:33:10Z, with each `-wal` and `-shm`, and on copies of 24 h of `report.json`.

## Findings

**1. An edit's tier takes no permit, by design and in every sample.** `executeTier` calls `capacity.running(files)` for a tier without a lease (`src/core/scheduler/tiers.ts:547`). `running` writes a mark with `want` 0 and takes no lock (`src/core/capacity/pool.ts`, `running`). C10 says the same: "An edit's tier ... runs at `runner.tierSize` without a permit". An edit's tier holds at most `runner.tierSize` files (default 4, cezar 4; `selectTier`). In the samples, no daemon with a `want` 0 mark held a lock (`permitsUnderEditOnlyMarks` 0). The W8 and W9 workers' daemons (`07ee1929`, `0b0387ed`) ran edit tiers in 142 of all 443 samples with no permit.

**2. The 23 October premise was a misread.** `2026-10-23.md` said that a 200-file "fast tier is edit work". "Fast" is the pool's lane (`PoolLane`, task 004-61), not the edit class. A 200-file tier is a backlog tier: `runner.backlogTierSize` is 200, and an edit's tier is 4 files. The same thing happened today. `52e58f23` ran no-checkpoint tiers of 200, 200, 53, 200 and 200 files from 11:53:25Z to 12:05:20Z (store runs `bc9b70ef`, `fd2f3ca6`, `92374ef8`, `3543e167`, `4bee7fe0`), with 15 permits.

**3. A saturated host does not shut the gate.** The busy samples have mean 22.8 busy cores and mean load 53.9 (p90 83.2, max 104.0). The gate's view, the fresh marks' cores, had p50 4.1 cores, and the gate was open in 89.5% of them. From 11:53 to 12:02, nice-0 work outside Squeal kept the host saturated. At 11:32 this was mostly a Codex session's direct `vitest run` in the cezar worktree `bf1af356`. In that time `52e58f23` held 15 permits and ran 15 test processes, which used 1.0 to 2.9 cores (mean per 5-minute bin). One sample, 11:55:38Z: load 104.0, 23.9 busy cores, 16 permits held, 15 by one worktree, Squeal 5.0 cores, gate 4.8 cores, open. At nice 10 the workers took little CPU from the nice-0 work. They held the pool, though: 134 busy samples had a waiter's mark, 79 of them while one worktree held 8 or more. The longest backlog waits were 256 to 275 s (`0b0387ed`, `07ee1929`). The gate works when Squeal is the load: at 12:03:49Z, at load 58.1, the same tier used 15.1 cores, and the gate shut near 16.

**4. The busy-loop load after 12:30 is not Squeal's.** From 12:30, `%nice` reached 19 cores with 2 or fewer permits held. `ps` showed `sh -c while :; do :; done` at nice 10. The W8 brief asks its worker for such load ("bounded loops"). The 12:30 and 12:35 bins count it in busy cores.

The model keeps each file's duration fixed, and a saturated host breaks that. In `fd2f3ca6` (146 of 200 files reported), the files' durations add up to 3,870 s in a 283 s wall. That is about 14 files at once on 1 to 3 cores. The model gives 325 s at 15 workers and 546 s at 8. With CPU as the bound, 8 workers would likely finish in about the same wall (inferred). When load falls during a tier, the model's cost is real: a tier keeps its grant, and it gets no more permits mid-tier.

## Recommendation for Squeal

1. **Do not cap edit tiers.** They hold no permit, and on a busy host they used at most 3.1 cores (p90 1.6). A cap to 2 workers would add up to 8.6 s (squeal p90) or 27 s (cezar p90) to an edit's delivery and save about a core. That runs against C10: "the trade favours time to delivery".
2. **Gate backlog permits on the host's busy cores.** In `gatedPermits`, start the cores sum at the larger of the marks' cores (as now) and the host's busy cores. The busy cores come from `/proc/stat` over the same refresh window, with `%nice` included, so Squeal's own tests count once. On a host busy with other work, a daemon then takes a permit only while all the grants are below half the slots: 8 on this host, so `52e58f23` would have got 7. On a host where Squeal is the load, nothing changes.
   - Cost to an edit's wait: 0 s. An edit's tier takes no permit and never waits (finding 1). It could also run faster beside fewer niced backlog workers. Edit cores per process had a median of 0.29 with 10 or more backlog processes, against 0.40 with fewer (56 and 82 samples, observational).
   - Cost to the backlog and `squeal run --all`: by the model, p50 +7 to +9 s and p90 +46 to +59 s per tier at 8 workers against 16. That cost applies only while the host is busy. When the CPU is the bound, the real cost is less (finding 5).
   - The tradeoff: catch-up speed on a busy host, a priority since 2026-10-19, against pool waiters (79 samples, waits up to 275 s) and memory (15 Vitest workers held while they used about 3 cores).
   - This amends the accepted 011-83 decision ("a gate on Squeal's measured process-tree CPU"). It is the human's call, not a repo-local one.
3. **Do not cap one worktree's share.** A fixed cap of half the slots per daemon would also slow a lone catch-up on an idle host, the common case. Fair shares already split the pool when several daemons want it.

## Open questions

- Should a held lease shrink when the host becomes busy during its tier? C10 says "a held permit is never taken back". `52e58f23` held 15 permits through tiers of 81 s to 283 s.
- How many busy cores should shut the gate: the slots (16 here) or the CPU count (24)? This window cannot decide it, because the host was at 23 to 24 busy cores or under 18.
- Is the pool-wait cost large enough to matter for C3? The waits were backlog waits, and this window does not show whether a file was lost to one.
